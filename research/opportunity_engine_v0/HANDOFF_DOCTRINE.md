## Core goal

Build the inside of MULTIVERSE before UI/avatar/gamification. The product-facing app, kingdom/avatar/rank visuals and other presentation are decoration that can be added later. The durable asset is the system that discovers opportunities, verifies them, compares alternatives, predicts several steps ahead, chooses an action, measures the result and learns.

The intended loop is:

world signals -> candidate discovery -> competitor/substitute research -> evidence verification -> AI-substitutability test -> economic simulation -> future-step / demand-death forecast -> leverage search -> choose monetization/exposure -> small reversible execution -> measured outcome -> learning dataset -> next decision.

## Non-negotiable doctrine

1. Research broadly, deploy narrowly. Many candidates may be researched in parallel; capital/time focus defaults to one winner at a time.
2. Competitor research is mandatory. A candidate cannot advance without direct competitors, substitutes, big-tech/general-AI replacement risk, and a credible explanation of why the opportunity is not already common.
3. Existing systems first. Prefer using existing/free services, general AI and service integrations. Build only the differentiated last layer that creates value. Provider choices must remain replaceable where practical.
4. Do not fight general AI with a thin AI wrapper. If ChatGPT/Gemini/general AI can solve roughly 80%+ of the problem, reject by default unless proprietary data, action completion, deterministic verification, integration, network/usage effects, AI-facing infrastructure or owned distribution creates a real edge.
5. Distribution is part of the product. A useful thing with no credible discovery/acquisition path is not a strong candidate.
6. Model at least three future steps; normally reason 3-7 steps ahead. Include how demand grows, how competitors react, when AI/incumbents commoditize the value, and when to exit.
7. Short-lived demand is acceptable when build/payback fits well inside the window. Do not overbuild. Entry timing and exit timing are equally important.
8. Never imply long-lived value when known demand is temporary. No fake scarcity, fake countdowns, hidden material limitations, deceptive cancellation friction, or other manipulative tactics.
9. Always compare low/base/high economics. Ask whether the low case still justifies the test.
10. Measure actual outcomes. Strategy scores without real traffic/conversion/profit/decay evidence are hypotheses, not truth.
11. The long-run moat should come from accumulated outcome data, historical change data, verified mappings, distribution, integrations, behavior/conversion history, reusable code and opportunity-discovery speed—not idea secrecy alone.
12. The owner-facing kingdom/chess/rank metaphor is optional UI only. Core terminology stays ordinary: profit, ceiling, risk, competition, demand life, automation, owner hours, exit trigger, etc.
13. Leverage search is mandatory for serious candidates. For each opportunity, actively search how distribution, automation, AI, monetization, data, reuse, geography, partners/platforms and infrastructure can increase output per owner hour or per yen at risk.
14. Do not call something leverage if it merely creates hidden workload, fixed cost, vendor lock-in, legal risk, inventory, sales-call dependence, team dependence or dangerous copy exposure.
15. Do not multiply claimed leverage factors naively. Effects overlap. Use conservative diminishing credit until measured outcomes replace estimates.
16. For serious distribution candidates, search for bidirectional growth loops, not only one-way funnels. Ask whether activity on one surface creates useful material or intent for another surface, and whether the return path creates genuine user value and measurable repeat cycles.
17. Cross-promotion is leverage only when the user has a real reason to move in both directions. Do not manufacture page views, spam, fake engagement or policy-violating promotional behavior.
18. Prefer at least one owned or portable capture point in a growth loop. External platforms are rented distribution; APIs, ranking, pricing, moderation and posting rules can change.

## Three economic horizons

### Tactical / short-wave layer

Examples: breaking news, natural controversy, influencer/athlete/idol attention, movie/game/music releases, tournaments, product launches, limited goods, stock-outs/restocks, collaborations, seasonal spikes, rule/spec changes and service shutdowns.

These may be worth entering even if demand will die soon. The engine must estimate the attention peak, purchase intent, build time, payback, competitor-arrival time and exit trigger. Short-wave opportunities can produce cash, data, distribution, reusable code or evidence for larger systems.

Do not create or amplify scandal, misinformation, defamation, privacy invasion or harassment. Use only lawful, verified, naturally occurring attention.

### Durable human-demand layer

Study persistent human needs such as food/convenience, rest/sleep, health, intimacy/relationships, belonging/status, entertainment/escape, money/security, appearance, learning, mobility and time saving. Historical industries—including very old ones—are useful as evidence of which underlying needs persist.

Do not confuse durable need with durable business format. Bars, physical stores, a specific app, a specific device or a specific interface can disappear while the underlying demand survives.

History should be used to ask:
- what underlying need persisted;
- what delivery mechanism died;
- what technology changed cost/access/speed/distribution;
- who captured the early value;
- what incumbents later commoditized;
- what durable asset remained.

### Enabling-wave / future layer

Continuously look for the next platform shift after PC -> internet -> smartphone -> AI. Do not promote hype based on one signal. Require multiple independent adoption indicators.

The key question is not merely "what technology is next?" but "which human capability or recurring need is being externalized next?" Candidate domains may include judgment, action, transactions, permissions/identity, embodied/robotic action, persistent ambient interfaces and collective intelligence, but these are hypotheses until adoption evidence exists.

The strongest higher-scale search space is:

persistent need x verified enabling wave x unmet last-mile gap x low owner burden x survivable competition.

## Market diffusion / immunity / evolution lens

The biological language is a research metaphor, not literal biology and not a justification for harmful dependence.

For each candidate, model:
- transmission: whether one user's use/attention creates more users;
- entry path: search, SNS/social, word of mouth, ads, marketplace, existing service, etc.;
- host fit: which group/country/use case is especially receptive;
- latency: time from awareness to use/payment;
- habituation immunity: how quickly familiarity/boredom reduces value;
- AI substitution immunity: how quickly general AI can neutralize the paid value;
- incumbent immunity: how quickly an incumbent/official/free feature can absorb it;
- mutation capacity: ability to change feature/use case/market and keep value;
- recurrence: whether new seasons/releases/rules/events recreate demand;
- network effect: whether group adoption increases value;
- embeddedness: how deeply it becomes part of a workflow/life;
- mutual value: whether continued use genuinely benefits the customer as well as the business.

Useful market patterns:
- ACUTE: fast spread, short life;
- SEASONAL: recurring bursts;
- LATENT: trigger/event causes delayed demand;
- CHRONIC: persistent workflow/need;
- SYMBIOTIC: durable use because both sides accumulate real value.

Prefer SYMBIOTIC over exploitative dependence. Harmful addiction, deception, dark patterns, misinformation, unsafe health effects or coercive lock-in fail closed.

The engine should detect rising immunity before the product dies, then consider mutation, migration to another segment, harvesting reusable assets, or exit. Do not keep a dying product alive for emotional reasons.

## Scale path / rank metaphor

Ranks are optional presentation metaphors only, but the economic idea matters:
- small tactical opportunities may generate quick cash/evidence;
- repeated winning patterns may become sustaining systems;
- sustaining automated systems may expand into growth/portfolio/platform economics;
- weak small opportunities should not be aggregated into fake scale if owner workload rises linearly.

Promotion requires evidence that revenue can rise without proportional owner labor. Track automation ratio, owner hours at ceiling, team/sales-call/inventory dependence, existing-system coverage, custom-build share, reversibility and ceiling blockers.

The owner remains the strategic authority. The UI may later represent business/system growth through kingdom ranks (hundred-person commander, thousand-person commander, general, great general, commander-in-chief, etc.), but the core must use actual economics and evidence.

## Leverage / force-multiplier layer

Every meaningful candidate gets a separate leverage scan after basic viability and competitor checks. The scan looks for ways to increase output without proportionally increasing owner labor or capital risk.

Mandatory leverage search areas:
- distribution: existing demand pools, search, marketplaces, social, referrals;
- automation: remove recurring owner work before hiring people;
- AI: use current models to reduce research/build/support/operation cost without becoming a thin replaceable wrapper;
- monetization: compare multiple revenue routes rather than forcing one business model;
- data: make each experiment or customer interaction create lawful reusable evidence;
- reuse: reuse code, workflows, integrations, content structure and distribution assets across opportunities;
- geography: look for country/language timing gaps and portable systems;
- partner/platform: use existing ecosystems/APIs/affiliate systems where they create reach or capability;
- infrastructure: rent commodity plumbing such as hosting, payments, analytics, email/notification and identity instead of rebuilding it when practical.

Reject leverage options that require dangerous disclosure of the core method, deception, high legal risk, teams, sales calls, inventory or other constraints incompatible with the owner model. Penalize vendor lock-in and recurring fixed costs. Prefer verified, reversible, low-cost options that compound automation, data, distribution or reusable assets.

Do not assume claimed 2x, 3x and 4x uplifts multiply to 24x. Use conservative diminishing credit until real measurements exist.

A future application may visualize opportunity/business units as commanders, leverage options as equipment/capabilities, go-to-market/exit choices as tactics, and repeated measured improvement as training. This is optional presentation only; the underlying decision uses normal economic and risk metrics.

See `leverage.py`, `test_leverage.py` and `LEVERAGE_DOCTRINE.md`.

## Bidirectional growth-loop layer

For every serious distribution opportunity, ask whether a useful two-way loop can exist between an owned/portable surface and an external discovery surface, or between multiple complementary surfaces.

Example pattern:

owned utility -> shareable result/content -> external platform -> discovered intent -> deep link/import/action -> owned utility -> new result/content.

The recipe-app / short-video example is only an illustration. The reusable asset is the loop pattern itself.

A one-way funnel is not a loop. A two-way path is still weak if either direction gives the user no real value. Measure transition friction, attribution, repeat-cycle rate, platform dependency, policy risk, owned capture and reusable asset growth.

Do not assume virality. External platforms are rented distribution. Verify current official platform rules before implementation and maintain a fallback path where practical.

See `growth_loop.py`, `test_growth_loop.py` and `GROWTH_LOOP_DOCTRINE.md`.

## Output exposure / selling slices of MULTIVERSE

MULTIVERSE itself does not need to be sold. The engine may choose among:
- execute internally;
- sell a time-bounded signal;
- sell a bounded playbook;
- provide limited access/licensing;
- use an output as free acquisition;
- retain internally;
- reject.

Compare expected internal profit with output-sale/licensing profit and the strategic cost of creating competitors or leaking advantage.

Lower strategic-value, short-lived outputs may be monetized externally when useful to a real buyer. Higher-scale opportunities, proprietary outcome data, full candidate maps, scoring methods and core learning logic are presumptively retained. The best mine map is not automatically for sale.

## Existing-service assembly principle

Avoid rebuilding commodity layers. Prefer replaceable external services for payment, hosting, automation, email/notification, analytics, marketplaces, affiliate infrastructure, identity and other generic plumbing when that is safer and cheaper. The custom MULTIVERSE layer should primarily own selection, verification, orchestration, policy, exposure choice, future-step reasoning, measured learning and proprietary data.

Vendor shutdown/price/policy risk must be modeled. Every important external dependency should have an exit or replacement path where practical.

## Monetization choice is a decision, not an identity

Do not decide in advance to be "an affiliate marketer", "an app maker", "an SNS operator" or "a reseller". For each opportunity choose the best route among advertising, affiliate, one-time sale, subscription, transaction fee, selective output sale/licensing, internal asset or no action.

Consider follow-on demand several steps ahead. The system may predict complementary purchases or later needs, but surface only the next genuinely useful one or two steps to the user. No deceptive urgency or forced additional purchase.

## Actual learning target

The strongest proprietary dataset is a history of:

signal/change -> prediction -> competitor state -> leverage chosen -> chosen action -> traffic -> conversion -> profit -> owner time -> decay time -> exit -> residual assets -> failure/success reason.

Over time this should answer questions such as:
- which signal combinations actually predict monetizable demand;
- how long after a news/event signal purchase intent peaks;
- when competitors typically arrive;
- when AI/official features erase the opportunity;
- which monetization route works for which opportunity class;
- which leverage options actually increase profit per owner hour rather than just adding complexity;
- which growth-loop transitions produce repeat cycles rather than one-off clicks;
- which external platforms create durable acquisition vs fragile rented reach;
- which small tests predict larger-scale winners;
- which apparent trends should be ignored.

Do not raise confidence merely because more theory was written. Confidence should rise with measured evidence.
