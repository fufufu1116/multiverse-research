# Opportunity Engine v0 — Existing-System-First Assembly

Status: RESEARCH PROTOTYPE ONLY
Runtime: OFF

## Principle

Do not rebuild commodity infrastructure.

Target architecture:
- >=80% of a candidate's delivery stack should be satisfied by existing systems when practical;
- <=20% custom code should concentrate on the differentiating decision/control/data layer;
- vendor examples are replaceable components, not canonical dependencies;
- availability, terms, pricing and permissions must be freshly reverified before live use.

## Three economic layers

### 1. Tactical cash layer
Short-lived opportunities: trend bursts, launches, product attention, public events, rule transitions.
Goal: fast validation and payback, explicit exit trigger, little or no inventory.
May remain intentionally small if it produces cash, reusable data, distribution or code.

### 2. Repeatable sustaining layer
Patterns proven across multiple tactical opportunities become repeatable systems.
Goal: credible path to user-configured sustaining monthly profit with low owner hours and high automation.
Do not assume many small opportunities automatically equal a durable business.

### 3. Scalable asset/platform layer
Only promote patterns with evidence of repeatability, proprietary outcome data, owned distribution, transaction/action completion or other accumulated advantage.
Goal: growth/portfolio/platform-class economics without requiring meetings, a large team or high-touch operations by default.

## Reuse map

Use existing services for commodity functions where they fit:
- signal sources: public/authorized search, trend, news, social, ad, marketplace and official-source feeds;
- workflow glue: automation/integration platforms or direct APIs;
- checkout: hosted/no-code checkout/payment links before custom payment UI;
- hosting/edge: managed/serverless hosting before dedicated infrastructure;
- analytics: existing web/product analytics before custom dashboards;
- affiliate/commerce: existing authorized affiliate/marketplace programs before custom merchant operations;
- messaging/audience: existing newsletter/notification platforms when an owned audience is justified;
- AI: provider-neutral use of existing models; do not build a foundation model.

Custom code should focus on:
1. candidate normalization;
2. mandatory competitor/substitute research gates;
3. AI-substitutability checks;
4. multi-step future/demand-death/exit reasoning;
5. low/base/high economics;
6. scale-path classification;
7. evidence/provenance and result history;
8. winner allocation and learning from realized outcomes.

## Scale rule

A small candidate is acceptable only when at least one is true:
- it can credibly reach the configured sustaining-profit threshold;
- it has a documented path into a higher-scale repeatable system;
- it is intentionally tactical but pays back fast and leaves reusable cash/data/distribution/code.

A candidate must not be mistaken for a durable livelihood when its credible ceiling is below the configured sustaining-profit threshold.

The higher layers are not assumed to come from merely stacking many weak tactical opportunities. Promotion requires evidence that a repeatable pattern exists and that scale does not secretly depend on proportional owner labor, sales calls, inventory or a team.

## Human-burden guardrail

Scale forecasts must explicitly reveal whether the forecast eventually requires:
- high owner hours;
- sales calls;
- a team;
- inventory;
- irreversible capital commitment.

Those are blockers/penalties under the current owner constraints, not hidden assumptions.
