# Cross-Market Clean Growth Doctrine

Status: research-only / non-authoritative. Runtime remains OFF.

## Purpose

The Opportunity Engine MUST apply the same clean-growth standard across domestic services, services for foreign residents or overseas users, multilingual products, creator/distribution products, SaaS, commerce, affiliate/referral, agency, managed-service, and other opportunity classes. No audience or geography is a reason to weaken legality, platform-policy compliance, user value, transparency, or evidence requirements.

## Core rule

A growth or monetization mechanism is stronger when it is:

- lawful in the relevant jurisdiction;
- permitted by the provider/platform contract and current policy;
- transparent about price, role, material limitations, recurring billing, cancellation and support boundaries;
- based on genuine user value rather than confusion, dark patterns, impersonation, coercion or policy evasion;
- measurable and reversible at small scale before larger commitment;
- resilient to one platform/provider changing ranking, API, price, terms or availability;
- capable of creating owned or portable assets such as customer relationships, consented first-party data, reusable content/processes, brand trust or interoperable exports.

## Foreign-user / cross-border extension

For a foreign-resident, visitor, immigrant, expatriate, multilingual or overseas-facing service, the engine MUST NOT treat language or jurisdictional complexity as an arbitrage loophole. Before advancement, research should identify as applicable:

1. target country/region and actual payer;
2. language and accessibility requirements;
3. local consumer-protection, advertising, privacy/data-transfer, tax/payment and licensing constraints;
4. platform/provider territorial restrictions and resale/referral/agency rights;
5. currency, payment failure, refund/chargeback and support burden;
6. cultural/localization needs beyond literal translation;
7. existing local competitors, substitutes, incumbents and general-AI substitution;
8. whether the service creates independent value instead of merely adding a markup to an unchanged upstream product;
9. whether a compliant affiliate, referral, agency, OEM/white-label or managed-service structure is safer than informal resale/account transfer;
10. an exit/hold trigger if rules, economics, demand or platform access deteriorate.

## Reciprocal distribution

The existing Growth Loop rules are market-agnostic. A foreign-user service can use reciprocal distribution only when both directions provide real user value. Examples may include multilingual content <-> owned service, community <-> utility, marketplace <-> workflow tool, or local guide <-> booking/transaction layer, but examples are hypotheses rather than adopted businesses.

A share button, translation, cross-post, affiliate link or imported audience alone is not a flywheel. `growth_loop.py` remains the deterministic gate: deceptive tactics and high legal risk fail closed; high platform dependency can remain fragile; owned/portable capture and measurable attribution are rewarded.

## Economics

Do not optimize gross spread alone. Compare contribution economics after upstream cost, payment fees, taxes/compliance, refunds/chargebacks, support/localization, tooling/automation, customer acquisition, churn and owner time. Cross-border complexity is a cost/risk term, not free leverage.

## Authority boundary

This doctrine does not authorize provider/API calls, credentials, purchases, advertising, publication, customer contact, collection of personal data, contracting, live business effects, Runtime activation, adoption or merge. Those remain governed gates.
