# Adaptation + Reciprocal Distribution Doctrine

Status: RESEARCH PROTOTYPE ONLY
Runtime: OFF

## Why this exists

Two recurring strategic patterns should be searched across opportunities rather than kept as industry-specific anecdotes.

1. Reciprocal distribution: surface A sends users to surface B, and useful activity on B naturally creates a reason to return to or redistribute through A. A share button alone is not a flywheel.
2. Constraint inversion: a regulation, platform-policy change, technology shift, price shock, shutdown or other external constraint breaks a fragile mechanism and forces a replacement that can be more compliant, resilient, transparent, cash-efficient or scalable than the old one.

## Reciprocal distribution rules

For every serious candidate, ask whether user activity can create a lawful, useful acquisition loop across existing platforms/services.

Evaluate:
- independent user value in both directions;
- friction in each transition;
- attribution/measurement quality;
- whether ordinary product use naturally creates distributable output;
- whether the loop can repeat without spam or manipulation;
- dependence on one platform algorithm/API;
- current platform policy and content/IP rights;
- whether the loop still has value if one external platform weakens.

Do not call one-way acquisition a flywheel. Do not use deceptive virality, forced sharing, spam, unclear content rights, fake engagement or platform-policy evasion.

The cooking example is only an example: recipe app -> useful short-form cooking output -> social discovery -> deep link back into shopping list/timer/cooking workflow -> new useful output. The reusable principle is the loop, not the recipe category.

## Constraint inversion rules

Treat external shocks as selection pressure, not as a loophole opportunity.

When an old mechanism is threatened, search for replacements that can improve:
- compliance confidence;
- collection/cash-flow quality;
- resilience;
- owner-hours burden;
- distribution reach;
- user trust/transparency;
- reusable data/process/software assets;
- optionality before the next shock.

A constraint inversion is attractive only when the replacement is independently lawful/useful and stronger on at least one important business dimension while improving resilience/compliance.

Never classify the following as innovation or positive adaptation:
- moving regulated or harmful conduct to a supposedly different jurisdiction/channel;
- evasion of law, platform policy or enforcement;
- coercive collection or harmful customer exploitation;
- deceptive sales or dark patterns;
- replacing one single-point dependency with a worse one.

## Seed options before crisis

The strongest adaptation often depends on options prepared before the shock. For durable candidates, maintain small reversible seeds where justified: alternate distribution, payment, hosting, model/provider, export format, data portability, or channel capability. Seeds should be cheap and tested; do not build large unused backup systems merely for appearance.

## Evidence discipline for industry anecdotes

Industry stories are hypothesis generators, not canonical facts. Separate:
- official/legal facts;
- independently measurable outcomes;
- company/person claims;
- industry-media estimates;
- interpretation and strategic analogy.

Do not hard-code disputed sales, market-size or causal claims into scoring logic. Preserve the general mechanism and require fresh evidence when evaluating a live opportunity.
