# Opportunity Engine v0 — Force Multiplier Doctrine

Status: RESEARCH PROTOTYPE ONLY  
Runtime: OFF

## Purpose

A promising opportunity is not complete when the base idea is scored. MULTIVERSE must separately search for the best safe combination of existing systems, distribution, automation, AI, monetization, data capture, reuse, geography, partners/platforms and infrastructure that can increase output per owner hour and per yen at risk.

The objective is not to attach every available tool. The objective is to choose the smallest complementary stack that creates the highest expected leverage while preserving reversibility, low owner burden, low fixed cost and protection of the core advantage.

## Loadout principles

1. Broad leverage search is mandatory before calling a serious candidate optimized.
2. Individually unsafe leverage options never become safe merely because the combined economics look attractive.
3. Evaluate combinations, not only isolated tools. A modest distribution lever plus strong automation may beat a flashy single AI tool.
4. Explicitly model complementarity and overlap. Two tools that solve the same bottleneck should not receive full additive credit.
5. Respect hard budgets for setup cash, setup time, recurring cost and owner hours.
6. Prefer diverse stacks only when the elements address different bottlenecks. Complexity itself has a cost.
7. Claimed multipliers receive diminishing credit until measured results exist; never naively multiply every vendor or model estimate.
8. High copy exposure, high legal risk, deceptive tactics, team dependence, sales-call dependence or inventory dependence fail closed under current owner constraints.
9. Prefer replaceable external services for commodity functions. Vendor lock-in is a penalty and important dependencies should have replacement paths where practical.
10. The best stack is provisional. Real traffic, conversion, profit, owner-time and failure data must eventually replace forecasted leverage.

## Tactics / order of operations

The same equipment can perform very differently depending on sequence. Default logic should therefore prefer:

validate evidence and payer -> prove a small conversion path -> automate recurring work -> add distribution -> scale only after unit economics are measured -> preserve data/reusable assets -> exit or mutate when demand/competition/AI absorption changes.

Do not buy scale before proving conversion. Do not automate a process that has not shown value. Do not overbuild for short-lived demand. Do not keep scaling after the exit condition has fired.

## Capability development

Training means improving the system through measurable experiments, not adding cosmetic points. Track capability in evidence quality, distribution, automation, monetization, data, reuse and forecasting.

Choose training actions that:
- target current weak links;
- have a measurable result;
- create reusable learning/data/code where possible;
- fit small cash/time budgets;
- do not expose the core method;
- do not require a team or high-touch sales;
- stay within legal and safety boundaries.

A capability is not considered improved because theory says it should be. Improvement should ultimately be supported by settled experiments and forecast-vs-outcome evidence.

## Existing-system examples are replaceable, not canonical

Current public options demonstrate that large parts of the stack can be rented instead of rebuilt: automation platforms can connect thousands of services; no-code payment links can handle one-time or recurring payments; low-cost edge/serverless platforms can provide small-scale hosting and execution. These are examples, not fixed vendor choices. Always re-check current price, limits, eligibility, policy and alternatives before real use.
